# UBC adoption plan

Start with evidence: a Ghostlight Personal agent asks a Ghostlight Business agent for service; the business applies its policy; the person approves any exception; both receive a traceable result. The independent simulated appliance then demonstrates owner-granted diagnosis and repair. The appliance prototype works locally; the two shipping Ghostlight apps still need their transport and consent adapters.

## First public milestone

Publish a tagged **developer preview**, quickstart, runnable demo, protocol limitations, test vectors and a short screen recording. Identify Ryan Sloan as UBC's creator and keep MIT licensing visible. Do not advertise production security, decentralized consensus, cross-vendor compatibility or completed Ghostlight app integration before those claims are tested.

Invite a small first cohort: one independent agent developer, one service-business software developer and one embedded-device developer. A successful integration by someone outside Ghostlight is stronger adoption evidence than announcement impressions.

## Announcement draft — for the working preview

> I’m Ryan Sloan, creator of UBC — Universal Bot Communication. I’m developing a free, MIT-licensed protocol for personal agents to communicate with business agents and, with owner approval, devices.
>
> The developer prototype includes signed identities, an encrypted relay conversation and a simulated appliance that requires separate permission for diagnosis and repair. It also explores owner-approved resale and a verifiable custody history.
>
> This is early work, not an audited production protocol. I’m looking for independent implementers and security reviewers to challenge the design and help test interoperability. The repository includes the demo and an explicit list of what remains unfinished: https://github.com/GhostlightAI/UBC

Do not use this as a claim that all currently staged files are already published. Link an actual tested release after publishing it.

## Where to share after the demo is reproducible

1. GitHub README, release notes and discussions: the durable canonical reference.
2. Ryan's LinkedIn or other established account: a short personal demonstration linked to the repository.
3. A relevant developer community's project-showcase channel, after reading its current posting rules. Publish one technical explanation and invite criticism; do not mass-post or contact members automatically.
4. Agent interoperability and embedded-device maintainers: approach with a concrete adapter or comparison, acknowledging A2A and FDO. Seek interoperability rather than claiming to replace established standards.

Track completed external quickstarts, independent implementations, tested interoperable exchanges, reproducible bug reports and active maintainers. Do not use stars or message volume as proof of adoption.

## Maintainer model

Keep the protocol, conformance tests and reference implementations public. Publish an RFC process, compatibility/version policy, implementation test matrix and a security reporting route. Protect release keys and use review on protocol changes. Invite maintainers beyond Ghostlight; do not make a central Ghostlight directory, paid token, exclusive relay or commercial account a requirement for independent use.

Hosting and support may cost money even when the protocol is free. Allow self-hosted and third-party relays, portable keys and explicit service charges without network-wide license fees.
